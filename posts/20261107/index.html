notes#

  • Ugly milkshake duck by Rafe Meager

Ur-Fascism by Umberto Eco: Disagreement is a sign of diversity. Ur-Fascism grows up and seeks for consensus by exploiting and exacerbating the natural fear of difference. The first appeal of a fascist or prematurely fascist movement is an appeal against the intruders. Thus Ur-Fascism is racist by definition.

Freedom and liberation are an unending task.

For my generation this question is irrelevant: we immediately understood the moral and psychological meaning of the Resistance. For us it was a point of pride to know that we Europeans did not wait passively for liberation. And for the young Americans who were paying with their blood for our restored freedom it meant something to know that behind the firing lines there were Europeans paying their own debt in advance.

If by totalitarianism one means a regime that subordinates every act of the individual to the state and to its ideology, then both Nazism and Stalinism were true totalitarian regimes.

If the Nazi architect was Albert Speer, there was no more room for Mies van der Rohe. Similarly, under Stalin’s rule, if Lamarck was right there was no room for Darwin.

Fascism became an all-purpose term because one can eliminate from a fascist regime one or more features, and it will still be recognizable as fascist. Take away imperialism from fascism and you still have Franco and Salazar. Take away colonialism and you still have the Balkan fascism of the Ustashes. Add to the Italian fascism a radical anti-capitalism (which never much fascinated Mussolini) and you have Ezra Pound. Add a cult of Celtic mythology and the Grail mysticism (completely alien to official fascism) and you have one of the most respected fascist gurus, Julius Evola.

I think the praise that people give you on the internet is real, and the opprobrium and stigma is real always also. The internet is real life. You can log off, but you can leave most situations in meatspace also. The fact that you can leave a situation does not make the situation unreal or false.

You are small and that is the reality. You are small no matter how big you feel.

It’s too easy to say the internet sucks, the phones suck. The internet sucks in exactly the same way that everything else sucks. The problem is not that the internet or the phones apps are uniquely awful but that they are awfully like everything and everywhere else, and they didn’t used to be. They used to be an escape — because they used to be, effectively, a subculture. Before the internet became the culture, or the culture took over the internet, it was on the television.

It would be an error, as I see it, to associate fascism with hatred.

Fascism trafficks in a politics of frustration and resentment. These are ordinary and mundane emotions, and that is why they are so effective. It is for that reason that fascism lies whisperingly close to the populace so very much of the time.

The outgroup is not hated, they are resented for existing. Certain people want to destroy nonbinary people, not because they hate us, but because they resent our existence and what it implies. Resentment is not related to hatred, it is not even a form of anger: it is a form of envy.

Anxiety plays a significant role here also. For the fascist. This can be hard to recognise in its guise beneath the frothing of extremists, but everyone likes to dress up. I think the real origin of fascism is a desire for safety and control so great that it begins to claim dominion over others. We all recognise the validity of using any measure necessary in service of self defense; fascism has its root in being threatened by life itself, thus making all manner of violence against it always sanctioned and necessary.

The problem is not hatred. I do not mind if people hate me. I don’t like it, but it doesn’t affect me very much. I hate a lot of people, and not always for excellent reasons, but that is my business. What matters is what I feel entitled to do with that hate.

Excessive desire for safety is far more dangerous because it feels so right and so righteous.

I don’t like that quote from Toni Morrison and various other people about how in real life goodness and good people are interesting and beautiful, while real life evil is boring and tedious, etc — I think this is a misreading of Arendt (but a popular one, and anyway maybe I’m wrong and Arendt did literally mean that) — and the problem with this take is that it isn’t true. Real evil is extremely exciting (in destruction instead of construction?).

And Jean Amery wrote about this: The banality of evil is a fantasy in itself. (At The Mind’s Limits, P25). Smashing things and people up is always exciting. The thing that is not exciting is the long slow slide into the consequences of the smashing.

book: Jean Amery, At the mind’s limits.

I think we argue about the Elon Musk nazi salute because we can’t argue about the Elon Musk USAID thing, because the meaning of the Elon Musk USAID thing is unequivocal, and we do not want to be intimate with that meaning.

“Since both permanent war and heroism are difficult games to play, the Ur-Fascist transfers his will to power to sexual matters. This is the origin of machismo (which implies both disdain for women and intolerance and condemnation of nonstandard sexual habits, from chastity to homosexuality). Since even sex is a difficult game to play, the Ur-Fascist hero tends to play with weapons - doing so becomes an ersatz phallic exercise.” (Eco, same again). I will put emphasis on non-standard: fascism is a homogenising impulse. Cf. safety and control.

The shrugging off of this burden in the conservative — the radical contention that what matters is not history but my opinion about history however unhinged and even fabricated it may be — is the start of fascism. Fantasy life is bizarre, charged, incoherent, ridiculous; it is extraordinarily dangerous to have it enter into politics more than a minimal amount.

It is we, the intellectuals, who foolishly believe a person should or kind of “must” have some kind of motivating coherent ideology or even some fuller set of thoughts and opinions and desires beyond pure and simple domination (“winning”).

Naivety about this is sustained in part because it is too horrifying to consider. One of the things i noticed about how abuse functions on a variety of scales is that the eagerness of the victim to believe they are NOT in an abusive situation carries quite a lot of the water.

“if [trans people, intellectuals, etc] behaved better, or conducted ourselves more thoughtfully, they wouldn’t hate and persecute us” man come on. some of you need to go into therapy/analysis tout suite. the reason fascists hate intellectuals is that they hate the idea of evidence. the reason they hate queer people is that they hate freedom.

Fascism is a horror of life and vulnerability. We have said that. Now it’s time to stitch the things together. I will start. Thus, let us see that the crime committed by the people of USAID was to see and care about vulnerability, and the crime committed by the recipients of USAID was to be vulnerable at all. Need, human need, our need for each other, our mutual interdependence, is abhorrent.

Dying, or more specifically being subject to death at an unknown and yet appointed hour, is the ultimate vulnerability. Such a process — that thing, as an experience — must be prohibited. Thus, fascism is a suicidal position that is taken up to avoid or combat failure and decay. Degeneration, ageing, contact with outside forces be they cultural or biological has to be strictly prohibited.

In fact the fascist right wing scorns and disdains Charlie Kirk for the same reason that they disdain the troops — he got killed, and the troops can die. Getting killed is weakness and failure.

One of the reasons hatred is actually not associated with fascism is that hatred requires caring about beliefs. But the fascist personality does not have beliefs, it has desires, or perhaps more accurately appetites, the thing that Freud called drives. It will profess to have whatever beliefs necessary or useful for the furthering of these drives, nothing more. For this reason fascists are not galled or ashamed by their own hypocrisy — it means nothing to them, what matters is the rapacious desire for power, and that is constant. We more lowly mortal humans are ashamed of our hypocrisy because we take our beliefs seriously, not just intellectually but emotionally they matter to us. We liberal democrats are particularly concerned with legitimacy; there is nothing for which the fascist cares less. If I can kick you, I kick you. That is all the legitimacy I need.
